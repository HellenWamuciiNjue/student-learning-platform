# Grade 5 Mathematics

## Topic 1: Place Value of Whole Numbers

### Learning Objectives

- Identify the place value of digits in numbers up to millions
- Read and write numbers in words and figures
- Understand the value of each digit according to its position

### Key Concepts
Every digit in a number has a place value depending on its position.

In the number 4,582,317:

- 7 is in the ones place (value = 7)
- 1 is in the tens place (value = 10)
- 3 is in the hundreds place (value = 300)
- 2 is in the thousands place (value = 2,000)
- 8 is in the ten thousands place (value = 80,000)
- 5 is in the hundred thousands place (value = 500,000)
- 4 is in the millions place (value = 4,000,000)

### Place Value Chart (from right to left)
Ones → Tens → Hundreds → Thousands → Ten Thousands → Hundred Thousands → Millions

### Reading Numbers
4,582,317 is read as “four million, five hundred eighty-two thousand, three hundred seventeen”.

### Important Tip
Always start from the right when identifying place values. Commas help group digits in threes.

### Summary
Place value tells us the real value of a digit based on its position. Knowing place value helps with reading, writing, comparing, and calculating with large numbers.

### Common Mistake
Confusing the place (position) with the actual value of the digit.

## Topic 2: Ordering and Comparing Whole Numbers

### Learning Objectives

- Compare whole numbers using greater than (>), less than (<) and equal to (=)
- Arrange numbers in ascending and descending order
- Use place value to compare numbers

### Key Concepts
To compare two numbers, start from the highest place value (left side).

**Example:** Compare 45,678 and 45,768

- Both have 4 ten thousands and 5 thousands
- Next digit: 6 hundreds vs 7 hundreds → 45,678 < 45,768

### Symbols

- means greater than
- < means less than
- = means equal to

**Ascending order** = from smallest to largest
**Descending order** = from largest to smallest

**Example**
* Numbers: 3,245 · 3,425 · 3,245 · 3,542
* Ascending: 3,245 · 3,245 · 3,425 · 3,542
* Descending: 3,542 · 3,425 · 3,245 · 3,245

### Summary
Always compare digit by digit from the left. The first different digit decides which number is larger.

### Common Mistake
Looking only at the number of digits or the last digits instead of starting from the highest place value.

## Topic 3: Rounding Off Numbers
### Learning Objectives

- Round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred and thousand
- Apply the rounding rule correctly
- Understand when to round up or stay the same

### Key Concepts
### Rounding Rule
Look at the digit immediately to the right of the place you are rounding to:

- If it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4 → round down (keep the digit the same)
- If it is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9 → round up (add 1 to the digit)

**Examples**
Round 3,647:

- To nearest ten → 3,650
- To nearest hundred → 3,600
- To nearest thousand → 4,000

Round 12,450 to nearest hundred → 12,500 (because the tens digit is 5).

### Summary
Rounding makes numbers easier to work with while staying close to the original value. Always check the next digit.

### Common Mistake
Forgetting to change all digits after the rounding place to zero.

## Topic 4: Addition of Whole Numbers
### Learning Objectives

- Add whole numbers up to 6 digits with and without regrouping
- Use column addition correctly
- Estimate sums by rounding

### Key Concepts
Write numbers in columns according to place value (ones under ones, tens under tens, etc.).

* Add from right to left. When the sum of a column is 10 or more, regroup (carry over) to the next column.

**Example**

  45,678
+ 32,945
_______
  78,623

### Estimation
Round each number then add to check if the answer is reasonable.
* 45,678 ≈ 46,000
* 32,945 ≈ 33,000
* Estimated sum ≈ 79,000 (close to 78,623).

### Summary
Always align place values, add column by column, and regroup when necessary. Estimation helps catch mistakes.

### Common Mistake
Forgetting to add the carried-over number.

## Topic 5: Subtraction of Whole Numbers
### Learning Objectives

- Subtract whole numbers with and without regrouping (borrowing)
- Use column subtraction correctly
- Check subtraction by addition

### Key Concepts
* Align numbers by place value. Subtract from right to left.

* When the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit, regroup (borrow) from the next higher place.

**Example**

  8,000
– 3,456
_______
  4,544

### Checking
Add the difference to the smaller number; you should get the larger number.

### Summary
Regroup carefully and always check your answer by adding.

### Common Mistake
Forgetting to reduce the digit from which you borrowed.
