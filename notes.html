1. Principles of Human Resource Management

Year 1 • Core Unit • HRM

Definition (Use this in exams)

HRM is the strategic and coherent approach to the management of an organisation's most valued assets - the people - who individually and collectively contribute to the achievement of organisational objectives. (Armstrong, 2020)

Key Functions of HRM

2. Organisational Behaviour: Motivation Theories

Year 2 • High Exam Weight • OB

What Examiners Want

Never write one theory alone. Compare two theories and apply to a Kenyan organisation.

1. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs (1943):
Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-Actualisation.
Limitation: People don't follow the order strictly. A Kenyan boda rider may seek esteem before safety.
2. Herzberg Two-Factor Theory (1959):
Hygiene Factors (prevent dissatisfaction): Salary, working conditions, job security.
Motivators (create satisfaction): Achievement, recognition, responsibility.
Key point: Increasing salary alone does NOT motivate long-term, it only stops complaints.
3. McGregor Theory X & Y:
Theory X: Workers are lazy, need control. Theory Y: Workers are self-motivated if trusted. Modern HR favours Theory Y.
Exam Tip - 20 Mark Question: "Discuss motivation theories" = Define motivation + Explain Maslow + Herzberg + Compare + Apply to Safaricom/KCB + Criticise + Recommend what manager should do.

3. Employment Law in Kenya

Year 2/3 • Must Know Acts • Law

Key Laws to Memorise

  • Employment Act, 2007: Main law - contracts, leave, termination, working hours.
  • Labour Relations Act, 2007: Trade unions, CBA, strikes.
  • Labour Institutions Act, 2007: Creates National Labour Board.
  • Occupational Safety & Health Act (OSHA), 2007: Safety at work.
  • Work Injury Benefits Act (WIBA), 2007: Compensation for injuries.

Critical Exam Facts - Employment Act 2007

IssueLaw
ProbationMax 6 months, can extend 6 months with consent
Annual Leave21 working days with full pay
Maternity Leave3 months (90 days) full pay
Paternity Leave14 days full pay

4. Recruitment & Selection

Year 2/3 • Practical Unit • Recruitment

Difference (Common Exam Trap)

Recruitment = Attracting many applicants (positive process). Selection = Choosing the best one (negative process - rejecting others).

Recruitment Process - 7 Steps

  1. Job Analysis → Job Description + Job Specification
  2. Advertising (Internal vs External)
  3. Shortlisting
  4. Selection Methods: CV, Interview, Aptitude Test, Assessment Centre
  5. Reference & Background Checks
  6. Job Offer & Contract
  7. Induction / Onboarding

Selection Methods Compared

MethodAdvantageDisadvantage
Structured InterviewFair, reliable, comparableTime-consuming
Unstructured InterviewFlexibleBiased, unreliable
Aptitude TestObjectiveMay not predict job performance
Exam Tip: If asked "Discuss challenges of recruitment in Kenya" mention: Nepotism/tribalism, fake certificates, high unemployment leading to 1000s of applicants, skills mismatch.

5. Performance Management

Year 3 • Core for HR Managers • Performance

Definition

Performance Management is a continuous process of setting goals, monitoring, coaching, appraising and rewarding employees to improve organisational performance. It is NOT just the annual appraisal form.

Methods of Appraisal

  • 360-Degree Feedback: Feedback from boss, peers, subordinates, customers.
  • Management by Objectives (MBO): Manager + employee set SMART goals together.
  • Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton): Measures Finance, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth.
  • Behaviourally Anchored Rating Scale (BARS): Rates specific behaviours.
🎯 First Class Evaluation: Appraisals often fail due to Halo Effect (one good trait = all good), Recency Effect (only remember last month), Leniency Bias. Recommend training appraisers + continuous feedback.

6. Training & Development

Year 2/3 • Training Needs • Training

Training = Short-term, for current job. Development = Long-term, for future role.

Training Process

  1. Training Needs Assessment (TNA) - Organisational, Task, Person Analysis
  2. Design Objectives
  3. Choose Method: On-the-Job (coaching, job rotation) vs Off-the-Job (seminars, e-learning)
  4. Deliver Training
  5. Evaluate using Kirkpatrick's 4 Levels: Reaction → Learning → Behaviour → Results
Exam Tip: In Kenya, mention NITA (National Industrial Training Authority) and Levy - employers pay training levy to fund training.

7. Compensation & Reward Management

Year 3/4 • Highly Testable • Compensation

Components of Total Reward

  • Direct: Basic salary, overtime, bonuses, commission
  • Indirect: NSSF, NHIF/SHA, pension, medical cover, housing allowance, company car
  • Non-Financial: Recognition, flexible hours, career growth

Key Theories

Equity Theory (Adams): Employees compare their input/output ratio to others. If unfair, they reduce effort or quit. This explains why salary secrecy fails in Kenyan firms.

Expectancy Theory (Vroom): Motivation = Expectancy x Instrumentality x Valence. Employee must believe effort leads to performance, performance leads to reward, and reward is valued.

🎯 First Class Point: Link pay to Kenya's minimum wage orders + Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) for public sector. Shows current awareness.

8. Strategic HRM & HR Analytics

Year 4 • Final Year / Project • Strategic

What is Strategic HRM?

Aligning HR policies with business strategy to gain competitive advantage. HR sits at board level, not just admin.

Models

  • Best Practice / Universalist: Certain HR practices always work (e.g. training, selective hiring) - Pfeffer.
  • Best Fit / Contingency: HR must fit strategy. Cost-leadership firm needs different HR than differentiation firm.
  • Resource-Based View (Barney): People are VRIN - Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Non-substitutable. This is why talent is competitive advantage.

HR Analytics - Modern Exam Favourite

Using data to make HR decisions: Turnover rate, Absenteeism rate, Cost per hire, Training ROI, Time to fill. Example: If turnover in a Nairobi bank is 25%, HR Analytics shows it's due to low pay vs competitors, not "lazy staff".

🎯 First Class Conclusion Template: "In conclusion, modern HR has shifted from personnel administration to strategic partner. Organisations that invest in people, use data, and comply with Kenyan labour law will outperform competitors."
